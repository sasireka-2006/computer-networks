📝 EXACT OBSERVATION YOU CAN WRITE
Observation
Three network conditions were studied using NS-3: normal transmission, congestion and packet error conditions.

In the normal condition, the link data rate was 10 Mbps and traffic rate was 5 Mbps with 0% error. The throughput remained approximately stable around 5 Mbps.

In the congestion condition, the link data rate was reduced to 1 Mbps while the traffic rate remained 5 Mbps. Since the traffic rate exceeded the link capacity, throughput was limited to approximately 1 Mbps and packet loss and delay increased significantly.

In the error condition, the link data rate was 10 Mbps and traffic rate was 5 Mbps, but a 5% packet error rate was introduced. The throughput decreased compared with the normal condition and packet loss increased.

Final inference:
The graphs demonstrate that throughput depends on available link capacity and packet transmission errors. When traffic exceeds link capacity, congestion causes severe packet loss and delay. When packet errors are introduced, packet loss increases and throughput decreases even though sufficient link capacity is available.
